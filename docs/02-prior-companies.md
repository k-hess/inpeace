# The companies that came before us

The question this answers: the companies that tried this space and didn't make it, did they try charging for the executor stage and fail, or did they never try? Web research, August 2026, sources at the bottom.

## Verdict

Consumer-direct executor and estate-settlement software is under-tried, not tried-and-failed. No company has charged consumers for the executor stage as software and then shut down. Every failure-adjacent case was an acquisition or absorption into a funeral or insurance company, not a bankruptcy. The survivors are all small and all bill the estate.

## Company by company

| Company | After-death product? | Charged consumers for it? | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cake | Thin, mostly content | No | Acquired by Foundation Partners Group (funeral conglomerate), Oct 2024. Runs as "Cake – Afterall". |
| Lantern | Yes, core feature | No, by choice. Their own writing says charging post-loss undercuts trust. Monetized pre-need plans and concierge. | Acquired by Wellthy, shut down Sept 2024. |
| Everplans | Yes (deputy unlock of a vault) | Bundled into a $75/yr pre-death subscription. The real business became a $2,500/yr advisor channel. | Acquired by NGL (2021), then Precoa, a pre-need funeral lead-gen company (2024). |
| Farewill (UK) | Yes. Fixed-fee £595 probate, cremation from about £1K. | Yes, as services | Revenue up 36% in FY2023, never EBITDA-profitable (–£4.2M). Sold to Dignity for £12.9M off a roughly £70M peak. Failure mode was cost-to-serve at a staffed services company, not lack of demand. |
| Empathy | Yes | No. About 99% B2B2C through insurers and employers. | Best-funded player: $162M raised, 8 of the top 10 US life insurers as customers. B2B2C looks like a design choice, not a retreat. |
| EstateExec | Yes | $99 flat, reimbursable from the estate | Alive since 2015, bootstrapped, no public scale data. |
| Atticus | Yes, software plus a fiduciary team | $175 to $499, reimbursable from the estate | Alive, modestly venture-backed, consumer-direct first. |
| GoodTrust | Yes | $149/yr | Alive, consumer plus bank and credit-union channel. |
| Settld (UK) | Death notification | Free to consumers, B2B data and referral model | Acquired by The Estate Registry, 2024. |
| Trust & Will | No, document generation | $200 to $500 for wills and trusts | Alive and the largest brand by search volume. Pre-need only. |
| NOKBOX | Physical binder for a next of kin | Yes, product sale | Alive. Owns most of the "inventory wall" search demand (15K/mo on the brand name). |

## What the pattern says

- Consumer-direct pre-need software has a long record of failure: Cake absorbed, Everplans left consumer, Farewill sold distressed. Nobody wants to contemplate their own death to buy a legal document.
- Nobody who died charged consumers for the after-death stage as software. Lantern kept it free on principle. Everplans bundled it. Cake barely built it.
- The ones doing it today (EstateExec, Atticus, GoodTrust) are small and alive, at $99 to $499 billed to the estate.
- Every survivor won on a name people trust and on distribution, not on category search.

## Counterpoints worth holding

1. Thin base rate. Nine or ten companies, most alive. Not enough failures to prove that pre-need is a graveyard and executor works. It could equally be that nobody has scaled either side yet.
2. Empathy's B2B2C choice is a signal. The most sophisticated player routes around consumer acquisition at the moment of loss. The read: paying intent exists, and capturing it directly is the hard part. That's a distribution problem, which is what this group is set up to solve, not a demand problem.

## Gaps

- Farewill: no public CAC or segment-level profitability.
- Empathy: whether they ever tried consumer-direct is unconfirmed either way.
- EstateExec, Atticus, GoodTrust: no public revenue or scale numbers. "Modestly alive" can't be upgraded to "proven" without direct outreach.
