What you told us, what we found

Four things this group said on the call. Each one held up, and got sharper.

You said: lead with the wall

Organize and access, first

Classic pre-need software keeps failing: Cake is dead, Everplans fled to B2B, Farewill went from a large valuation to a distressed sale. What this group described is different, an access-and-inventory layer, and nobody sells that. One inventory serves all three doors.

You said: guidance, not vendor picks

Confirmed, with legal weight

A Place for Mom drew a Senate Aging Committee investigation for commission-linked rankings. Angi took an FTC order up to $7.2M. We teach incentive structure, fee-only versus commission, and never name providers.

You said: warm, not clinical

It's already built

"It's not 'I know what to do, I don't know how,' it's 'I don't even know what I need to do.'" — Kelly

The care-note guardrails and the "it can wait" section, given the same visual weight as the to-dos, are already live.

You said: feature-complete, or abandoned

One structure, three doors

Jordan's worry was correct, a thin checklist gets abandoned. One inventory object serves as a gathering list in planning mode and a notify-and-transfer list after a death. Same object, different verb. That reads feature-complete without the weight of three products.

The BabyList question

Jordan's benchmark, taken seriously.

What transfers

  • The claim mechanic. Claim, 48-hour soft hold, auto-release if unconfirmed.
  • Cash funds routed peer-to-peer. Zero platform cut, no fee line. We link out; we never touch the money.

What doesn't

  • The timeline. BabyList gets 9 months of joyful browsing. Death purchases happen in 24 to 72 hours, once, under duress.
  • The $750M. Retail, advertising, and insurance-billed product, none of which exist in grief. The honest comparison is Ever Loved, at $3M.

Yes, with a ceiling. Affiliate revenue on non-deadline goods in Phase 2. Caskets on a deadline, never.
